What is M3U8?

Files with the .m3u8 extension are plain text playlist formats encoded in UTF-8. They do not store video or audio content themselves, but only record access addresses of various media resources for players to read and load corresponding audio and video content.

It has a wide range of daily use cases: it can be used for online audio source mounting of internet radio stations, or manually created locally to organize exclusive music and video collection lists. With the m3u8 list, you can play your favorite audio and video resources in batches with one click, eliminating the trouble of opening files one by one.

The file can associate individual media files, entire resource folders through absolute paths, relative paths, or network URLs, and can also add text comments to label content notes. Initially, this format was only used for audio playback, such as MP3 track lists, but now it is widely used in video scenarios such as online short videos and live streaming media, and mainstream playback software is basically fully compatible with the m3u8 format.

How to Open M3U8 Files?

Any text editor such as Notepad that comes with your computer can directly open and view the internal code and resource links of m3u8. However, text tools only support viewing and editing, cannot directly decode and play audio and video content, and cannot replace professional players.

To properly play m3u8 streaming media or local list files, you can use professional audio and video software such as VLC or iTunes; Linux systems can use XMMS, and Mac devices recommend CocoModX, multiple tools can smoothly adapt to this format.

In addition, you can also directly use an online m3u8 player for quick parsing and playback. It should be noted that online tools only support m3u8 addresses in the form of network URLs, cannot read locally stored m3u8 files, and require the corresponding streaming media resources to be normally online and accessible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the M3U8 protocol?

It is an HLS streaming media indexing protocol developed by Apple Inc., mostly used in network video distribution and live broadcast scenarios. It splits videos into multiple small TS segments, records the segment addresses and order through m3u8 index files, and clients load and play them on demand.

How to play M3U8 video files?

Use a player that supports streaming media (such as VLC), browser extensions, or dedicated online parsing tools to directly open the link for playback. This tool is a free online M3U8 player, just enter the address to play.

What are the advantages of the M3U8 format?

Segmented loading, less buffering, mobile compatibility, strong network speed adaptability (can automatically switch different bitrates), easy anti-leeching, supports live broadcast and on-demand playback.

What's the difference between M3U8 and regular video formats?

It is not a complete video but a list of segment addresses; regular formats like MP4 are complete single video files. M3U8 is suitable for network transmission, while regular videos are suitable for local storage.

What are the common video playback formats?

MP4, FLV, MOV, AVI, MKV, WMV, WebM, etc. Among them, MP4 is currently the most universal format, and WebM is an open-source and free format.

How is M3U8 used in the live broadcast field?

During live broadcasts, the server will real-time slice the video into TS segments and continuously update the m3u8 index file. The client continuously pulls the latest segments to achieve low-latency online live broadcasting.
